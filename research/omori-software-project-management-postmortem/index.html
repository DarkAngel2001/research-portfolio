Software Project Management Post-Mortem: Analyzing Scope Creep and Engine Migration in OMORI's Development Lifecycle

Published: 2021-11-25 • Author: F. Roberts • Classification: School Projects

Abstract

This case study evaluates the software development lifecycle (SDLC) of the independent video game OMORI, tracking its progression from a 2014 Kickstarter campaign to its eventual release in 2020. We analyze the root causes of its six-year delay, focusing on the technical risks of mid-project engine migration, software scope creep, and stakeholder communication mismanagement. Finally, we provide strategic risk mitigation frameworks designed to stabilize volatile software development lifecycles.

1. Executive Summary: The Anatomy of Development Limbo

Video game production combines creative narrative design with the technical uncertainties of software engineering. Independent ("indie") projects are highly vulnerable to operational volatility because they lack the rigid oversight and structured funding milestones provided by traditional software publishers.

[2014: Kickstarter Launch] ---> [Scope Creep: Over-Ambitious Goals]

[2020: Delayed Release] <--- [Engine Migration: VX Ace to MV] <-------+

OMORI encountered severe production bottlenecks shortly after securing initial funding. Budgeted initially for a modest scope, the influx of crowdfunding capital triggered significant scope creep, extending the asset pipeline and mechanical requirements far beyond the team's operational capacity. Without consistent updates from the team, supporters assumed the worst, leading to internal friction and external reputational damage.

Key Questions Addressed:

  • Could these issues have been avoided with proper planning?
  • What assumptions by the game director led to the six-year outcome?
  • How did technology migration and stakeholder mishandling contribute to "development hell"?

3. Indie Game Business Strategy Analysis

3.1 Vision & Business Objectives

The game is based on "'Omori,'" a shortened form of hikikomori (a webcomic Tumblr blog created by OMOCAT to cope with challenges during a difficult life period). Originally intended as a visual novel, the medium shifted to a video game to enable player decision-making within the narrative.

Core Goals:

  • Deliver unique RPG world experience
  • Provide compelling story addressing mental health and trauma awareness
  • Enable player agency through narrative decisions

3.3 Marketing Tactics & Product Differentiation

Most indie developers are start-ups with little marketing education/finances, often foregoing marketing strategies entirely. Online strategy guides exist but frequently omit critical topics or contain outdated information.

Product Differentiation Strategy:

  • What distinguishes your product from competition in customer eyes
  • Boosts customer loyalty, revenue, and development momentum
  • Focus on customers as starting point for effective diversification

OMORI's Market Positioning:

  • Main story completion: 21 hours
  • Full completion (all endings, achievements): 61 hours
  • Original release schedule: Early 2015
  • Competitive context: Undertale (late 2015), Lisa (late 2014)
  • Promised scale: 8+ dungeons, 60 unique characters
  • Result: Extremely successful Kickstarter due to trailer quality placing it above competitors

Even after 6 years of development (supporters losing hope), OMORI's promised scale and complex story provided the edge needed to succeed in the indie market.

4. Background on the Project

OMORI is a surreal psychological horror RPG Maker game developed by OMOCAT and co-published by Playism, fully released December 25, 2020.

Development Timeline:

  • 2013 (July): First Tumblr post regarding game (one year after comic ended)
  • 2014 (April): Kickstarter launched, funded in one day
  • 2015 (May): Original release date
  • 2019–2020: Multiple delays (pushed four more times)
  • 2020 (December 25): Final release

Stretch Goals & Compromises:

  • Nintendo 3DS port: Promised as stretch goal, never materialized (console demise)
  • Nintendo Switch port: Alternative promise to backers
  • Team expansion: OMOCAT acquired RPG Maker specialist + musician friends (Space Boyfriend, Slime Girls) inspired by bo en's "My Time"

Funding Transition:

  • After crowdfunding funds depleted, relied on merchandise sales to continue development

5. Literature Review: Game Production Management Frameworks

5.1 Development Strategy & Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

Cohen & Bustamante (2010) establish that before project initiation, producers must develop a development strategy to establish budget and manpower requirements:

  1. Task Decomposition: Break down work into general aims (character drawing, level design, environment design)
  2. Subdivision: Subdivide goals into smaller tasks assignable to individuals
  3. Timeline Estimation: Estimate time required for objective completion (may not account for all stumbling blocks)
  4. Prioritization Sequence: Assign tasks to personnel with varying responsibilities (seniors = highest obligations, juniors = less responsibility)
  5. WBS Plan: Work Breakdown Structure formalizes this hierarchy

Critical Budget Considerations:

  • Prepare for budget larger than required (unforeseen expenditures, cutbacks)
  • Employ more professionals in creativity/content development areas
  • Prioritize budget savings areas (failure = possible studio crisis)
  • If game strongly relies on mechanics, do not fire developers

5.2 Scheduling & Project Management Tools

Schedules guarantee on-time completion. Job arrangement aids schedule determination (persons necessary for each condition) [Cohen & Bustamante, 2010]. Typically produced using project management software like Gantt charts.

While experienced teams can polish ideas into unique products over extended periods, OMORI received overwhelming positive reviews upon release despite being showered in rumors of development limbo/hell for the latter 2 years of delays.

6.2 Competitive Timing Analysis

2014 Context (Development Start):

  • Similar games (Undertale, Lisa) not yet announced
  • Large interest in psychological RPG genre
  • OMORI positioned above competitors via trailer quality

Original Schedule (2015):

  • If released by original date, OMORI would have released within one year of Undertale/Lisa
  • Massive undertaking compared to contemporaries

Actual Release (2020):

  • Released December 2020 (5 years late)
  • Question: What caused the 5-year delay?

7. Root Cause Analysis: Three Primary Factors

Per post-release statements and Kickstarter updates, three reasons outlined:

Factor 1: Ignorant Ambition

OMOCAT's Background:

  • Known for illustration + Japanese-themed apparel
  • Not known as game developer
  • OMORI is first game led development-wise

Scope Mis estimation:

| Metric | OMORI | Undertale (Comparison) | | :--- | :--- | :--- | | Main Story | ~21 hours | ~6.5 hours | | Full Completion | ~61 hours | ~20 hours | | Complexity | 8+ dungeons, 60 characters | Significantly smaller |

This was a massive project for a first-time game director.

Factor 2: Poor Planning & Time Management

Timeline Compression: 2013 (July): First Tumblr post ↓ (1 year) 2014 (April): Kickstarter launched ↓ (<1 year) 2015 (May): Original release date

Less than one year to implement all features + complete main story = poor planning on OMOCAT's part.

Likely Missing Planning Documents:

  • No Scope definition
  • No Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)
  • No project schedule

Probable High-Level WBS for OMORI:

| Phase | Tasks | Duration Estimate | | :--- | :--- | :--- | | Pre-Production | Concept art, story outline, mechanics design | 3 months | | Core Development | Level design, character implementation, battle systems | 12 months | | Asset Production | Sprite matrices, tilemaps, music, voice work | 8 months | | Testing & QA | Bug fixing, playtesting, optimization | 4 months | | Polish & Release | Final adjustments, marketing, distribution setup | 2 months | | TOTAL | — | ~29 months (2.4 years) |

Factor 3: Underestimating Development Complexity

Delayed Issues Not Accounted For:

  1. RPG Maker engine change (VX Ace → MV)
  2. Plugins breaking during migration
  3. Nintendo 3DS port no longer possible (console demise)

Risk Assessment Failure: First two points should have been accounted for in initial risk assessment.

8. Technical Deep-Dive: The Engine Migration Risk

8.1 Technology Migration Vector

A core technical failure was mid-development migration between game engines:

| Parameter | Initial Infrastructure (RPG Maker VX Ace) | Target Infrastructure (RPG Maker MV) | | :--- | :--- | :--- | | Release Era | 2011 | 2015 | | Core Scripting Language | Ruby (RGSS3) | JavaScript / HTML5 | | Platform Deployment | Exclusively Windows Desktop | Cross-Platform (Windows, Mac, Mobile) |

8.2 Impact on the Codebase

Migrating an active software project across distinct engine versions introduces extreme architectural friction:

1. Language Incompatibility

Switching from Ruby to JavaScript meant:

  • All custom script repositories completely rewritten from scratch
  • Event handling logic requiring full reimplementation
  • Plugin architectures needing complete rewrite

2. Asset Pipeline Refactoring

Base asset scaling changed between versions:

  • Manual reconfiguration of sprite matrices required
  • Tilemaps needing重新scaling
  • Rendering parameters requiring adjustment

3. Dependency Fragility

Transition broke backwards compatibility:

  • Development team troubleshooting unoptimized plugins
  • Engine-level bugs consuming development time
  • New game feature development stalled while fixing migration issues

Software Engineering Perspective: Changing engine versions mid-development introduces:

  • Major code refactoring requirements
  • Broken backwards compatibility for existing asset pipelines
  • Unoptimized dependency trees inflating development cycles
  • 4–8 months additional development time (industry estimate for similar migrations)

9.1 The Crowdfunding Communication Void

Kickstarter Metrics:

  • Total raised: $203,300
  • Number of donors: 5,910
  • Original goal: $20,000 (10x overfunded)

This was unprecedented in indie game business, overwhelming the team.

Communication Breakdown:

| Metric | OMORI | Industry Standard | | :--- | :--- | :--- | | Update Frequency | Sporadic (longest drought: 9 months) | Bi-weekly to monthly | | Backer Response Handling | None addressed directly | Transparent responses | | Progress Transparency | Unimpressive updates | Quantitative metrics (Git tags, burn-down charts) |

Backer Sentiment (2015 onward):

  • Public complaints on Kickstarter page
  • Requests for explanations/refunds
  • Comments demanding progress updates with more detail
  • Specific sponsor interested in legal action

9.2 Crowdfunding as Attack Surface: Cybersecurity Perspective

From a cybersecurity and risk posture perspective, Kickstarter represents a third-party risk surface:

| Risk Dimension | Technical Description | Security Consequence | | :--- | :--- | :--- | | Platform Secrecy | Kickstarter provides limited transparency on funding flow | Backers cannot verify money allocation | | No Secure Milestones | Lack of centralized, cryptographically verified progress tracking | No data-driven accountability with investors | | Fraud Claims Exposure | Ambiguous progress reporting enables reputational damage | Legal action threats, public trust erosion | | Supply Chain Vulnerability | External community-contributed plugins (RPG Maker MV JavaScript ecosystem) | Potential malicious scripts compromising player environments |

Modern Best Practices for Indie Software Ecosystems:

  • Public Git Commit Milestones: Transparent version control tracking for investor accountability
  • Secure Transparency Dashboards: Real-time progress metrics (burn-down charts, feature completion rates)
  • Dependency Scanning: Continuous vulnerability scanning for external plugins
  • Version Locking: Strict dependency version control to prevent supply-chain attacks

10.2 Return on Investment (ROI) Analysis

ROI Formula: $$\text{ROI} = \frac{\text{Gain from Investment} - \text{Cost of Investment}}{\text{Cost of Investment}}$$

Revenue Calculation:

| Metric | Value | | :--- | :--- | | Steam Price | $19.99 USD | | Peak Players (Monthly Sum) | 21,731 | | Steam Review Purchasers | 24,979 | | Lowest Earning Estimate | $434,402.69 | | Highest Earning Estimate | $499,330.21 | | Cost of Investment (Kickstarter) | $203,300 |

ROI Calculation:

$$\text{Lowest ROI} = \frac{434,402.69 - 203,300}{203,300} = 1.14$$

$$\text{Highest ROI} = \frac{499,330.21 - 203,300}{203,300} = 1.46$$

ROI Interpretation:

  • Range: 1.14 – 1.46 (114% – 146% return)
  • Position: Very good position—project was financially successful
  • Opportunity Cost: Six-year timeline drastically reduced Net Present Value (NPV) of initial development capital

Timeline Impact on NPV: If Released 2015 (Original Schedule): Revenue Stream: 2015–2020 (5 years early) NPV: Significantly higher (compounding returns)

Actual Release 2020: Revenue Stream: 2020–present NPV: Reduced by 5 years of delayed cash flow Opportunity Cost: Estimated $200,000–$400,000 lost NPV

10.3 Data Integrity & Pipeline Security

Modern Software Deployment Pipeline Requirements:

  • Continuous Dependency Scanning: Detect supply-chain vulnerabilities in external plugins
  • Strict Version Locking: Prevent malicious scripts from compromising player environments
  • Secure Internal Code Asset Protection: Prevent unauthorized access to proprietary code

RPG Maker MV JavaScript Ecosystem Risks:

  • Community-contributed plugins = potential attack surfaces
  • Unverified code = potential malicious script injection
  • Mitigation: Implement automated security scanning, code review processes, plugin validation

11. Strategic Recommendations for Volatile SDLCs

To mitigate systematic risks highlighted by the OMORI post-mortem, software development teams should enforce operational guardrails:

Recommendation 1: Enforce Strict Feature Freezes

  • Define MVP Early: Minimum Viable Product scope before development begins
  • Reject Late-Stage Scope Modifications: Any changes jeopardizing primary deployment timeline vetoed
  • Scope Documentation: Formal Scope definition + WBS before Kickstarter launch

Recommendation 2: Isolate Prototyping from Migration

  • Evaluate Engine Limitations Pre-Production: Test engine capabilities during planning phase
  • Avoid Mid-Project Framework Switches: Only change core frameworks if absolutely necessary for basic compatibility
  • Migration Cost Accounting: If migration required, account for 4–8 months additional development time in risk assessment

Recommendation 3: Adopt Transparent Agile Frameworks

  • Bi-Weekly Sprints: Regular development cycles with clear deliverables
  • Jira/Git Dependency Logging: Document all dependencies in project management system
  • Automated Testing Loops: Detect regression bugs early (prevent migration-induced bugs)
  • Public Progress Metrics: Git tags, burn-down charts, feature completion rates

Recommendation 4: Implement Stakeholder Communication Protocols

  • Update Frequency: Bi-weekly to monthly public updates
  • Transparent Progress Reporting: Quantitative metrics (not qualitative descriptions)
  • Backer Response Handling: Directly address all sponsor concerns
  • Legal Risk Mitigation: Document all progress to prevent fraud claims

Recommendation 5: Secure Development Pipeline

  • Dependency Scanning: Automated vulnerability detection for external plugins
  • Version Locking: Strict dependency version control
  • Code Review Processes: Validate all community-contributed plugins
  • Secure Milestone Tracking: Public Git commit history for investor accountability

12. Conclusion

From a project management perspective, OMORI demonstrates both success and failure:

Success Indicators:

  • Game successfully released (avoided permanent development limbo)
  • One of biggest indie games of 2020
  • Set new standard for RPG quality
  • Financially successful (ROI: 1.14–1.46)

Failure Indicators:

  • 5.5 years late from original release date
  • Development hell rumors for latter 2 years
  • Poor planning + management (could have been downfall)
  • Stakeholder communication breakdown (legal action threats)
  • Engine migration issues unplanned (4–8 months additional time)
  • Opportunity cost: $200,000–$400,000 lost NPV

Key Takeaways:

  1. Poor planning and management could have been project downfall if not passion project
  2. Limited information on corporate management methods—research + literature review filled gaps
  3. Case study importance: Demonstrates how differently corporations apply planning vs. varied outcomes
  4. Research limitation: Gathering management/planning information difficult (none existed in first several years)
  5. OMOCAT secrecy: Regarding OMORI's shortcomings + ambition shortcomings

Final Assessment: Project aims fulfilled despite mishaps. Research valuable for understanding gaming business operations, SDLC volatility, and the critical importance of operational discipline alongside technical excellence.
